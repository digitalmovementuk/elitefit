# Elite Fit Bali: voice guide for homepage v3

Version: v3.0 — 2026-10-02 · lane: voice (Fable 5.1) · for the copy writer, who sees only this guide, FACTS.md and the slot list. Everything quoted here is marked as **club voice** (the club's own pages, reels and film), **member voice** (verified Google reviews) or **Raoul's brief**. Nothing in this guide is page copy.

## 1. Raoul's brief, verbatim

## WHO IT IS FOR, AND HOW IT SOUNDS (Raoul is a member and knows the club first hand; his brief is the first source)
- The club: everything feels personal. It is a space where people can be themselves. It has everything to perform and reach goals, but it is not centred on performance and not about showing off. It is for expats and long-stay residents who want a home in Bali.
- So the page speaks like a welcoming place, not like a performance brand: personal and direct (talks to one person), calm and unforced confidence, community and belonging ("a place to call home" is the club's own line in its reel caption and "Better Together" film), practical for people who live here (flexible passes from a day to 52 weeks, partner discounts around Canggu, opening hours, WhatsApp with real people, real coaches by name), goals supported without theatre.
- Voice guard: no hype, no status, no competition words ("elite athlete", "beast", "crush", "shred", "no excuses", "exclusive", "luxury", "VIP", transformation promises); the word Elite appears only in the product and pillar names and is never used as a boast. No clinical, testing or performance-outcome claims, no new superlatives or promises.
- VOICE-GUIDE-ELITEFIT.md (+ html): Raoul's brief verbatim at the top, then evidence: the club's own pages, reel captions, and the verified Google reviews' wording (member language), then do and don't rules and ten example lines. From The Performance Lab only the FORM carries over: brevity, rhythm, the way labels, buttons and numbers are written and typeset; never its register or wording.

From the hero section of the same brief:
- Hard but warm: the hero keeps the Lab's energy, while the footage shows rooms and people being themselves, not performance posturing.

## 3. The voice

Elite Fit speaks like the person at the front desk who knows your name: direct, warm, unhurried and specific. It talks to one person, in the present tense, about what is here and what you can do with it today. Confidence sits in plain facts and short sentences, never in adjectives. Belonging is shown through people, names, routines and shared rooms, not claimed with a slogan. Goals are respected and supported; nothing is dramatised.

1. One person, present tense. Before: "Members enjoy access to our facilities from 6 AM." After: "You can train from 6:00."
2. Facts carry the confidence, adjectives do not. Before: "State of the art equipment in Bali's best gym." After: "Arsenal Strength, Panatta and Prime on the floor."
3. Belonging is shown, not declared. Before: "Join the Elite family and feel the community vibe." After: "A place where your name and your routine are known."
4. Practical beats poetic. Before: "Your sanctuary awaits whenever you need it." After: "Open 6:00 to 22:00, seven days. A day pass or up to 52 weeks."
5. Calm, never urgent. Before: "Don't miss out, spots fill fast. Book now!" After: "Book a class in the app or at the front desk."

## 4. Do and don't

### Do
- Person: "you" to the visitor. "We" is the team at the desk, used sparingly and only when a person could say it. Coaches by first name as the club writes them (Rey, Ain, Icha, Martijn, Putu); never "our experts".
- Tense: present. Future only for what happens after a tap ("We reply on WhatsApp"), never "you will feel", "you will become".
- Length: body sentences 6 to 14 words, one idea each. A section lead is at most two sentences. A list item is 2 to 6 words, no full stop. A label is 1 to 3 words.
- Numbers in prose: digits, always. Prices "IDR 350.000" (dot as thousands separator, as the club's price list); never "350k" on the page. Times 24-hour, "06:00" and "22:00" in readouts and labels, "from 6:00 to 22:00" in a sentence. Temperatures with unit and en dash: "4–6 °C". Durations: "1 hour", "4 weeks", "52 weeks"; in prose "a day" and "a week" are fine. Days: "seven days a week" or "every day". Rating: "4.7 on Google · 488 reviews".
- Community and belonging: show it with names, routines and shared rooms (classes, the cafe, the pool deck, the desk). Borrow the club's own form, verb plus "together" ("train together, eat together"), rather than the noun "community". One belonging line per section at most. The film's "your name, routines and goals are known" is the model.
- Practical for people who live here, only with facts that FACTS.md carries: the pass range from a day to 52 weeks and what each tier includes; partner discounts around Canggu with the membership length the data gives; opening hours; WhatsApp answered by the team; coaches by name; booking in the app or at the front desk; a day pass credited if you join afterwards. Never invent response times, distances, parking, air-conditioning as a club statement, personal-training prices, menu items or a dietitian schedule.
- Goals: name them as the visitor's and say what is here for them ("coaches, classes and a quiet pool afterwards"). No promise about the outcome.
- "Elite" only inside names: Elite Fit, Elite Fit Bali, the Elite tier, Elite Nutrition, Elite Training, Elite Rest. Never lower-case "elite" as an adjective, never "the elite choice", never a pun on it.
- Nothing internal on the page: no "prototype", "direction", "v3", "SEO", "keyword", "landing page", "placeholder", "TBC".

### Don't (banned words and ideas)
- Raoul's list: "elite athlete", "beast", "crush", "shred", "no excuses", "exclusive", "luxury", "VIP", transformation promises.
- Hype and status: "premium", "high-end", "world-class", "Bali's best", "#1", "unparalleled", "game-changer", "state of the art", "cutting edge", "next level", "level up", "unleash", "limitless", "ultimate".
- Competition and grind: "dominate", "warrior", "grind", "hustle", "beast mode", "earn it", "push past your limits", "test your limits".
- Transformation and outcomes: "transform", "transformation", "results", "burn", "shred", "lean", "sculpt" (outside a class name the timetable gives), "before and after", "lose", "gain", "guaranteed".
- Clinical and testing: "optimise", "biohack", "boost your metabolism", "detox", "recovery science", "performance data", "protocol", any health benefit of sauna, ice or steam.
- Urgency and guilt: "don't miss out", "limited spots", "only today", "last chance", "no more excuses", "what are you waiting for".
- Spa cliches: "sanctuary", "haven", "escape", "oasis", "pamper", "indulge", "bliss".
- Slogan belonging: "family", "tribe", "squad", "vibe", "like-minded", "community of" as a noun phrase, "where everyone knows your name" (a sitcom line, not the club's).
- Punctuation: no exclamation marks, no all-caps words for emphasis (the type system decides case), no ellipsis for suspense, no emoji.

## 6. Form rules (from The Performance Lab: form only, never register or wording)

Translated from the measurement lane's COPY-FORM-LAB.md v1.0 (research/lab/system/rest/, 2026-10-02 10:26). The Lab's form is hard and tight; Elite Fit keeps the tightness and fills it with human words. Where the two fight, the softening is written next to the rule. Where section 4 and this section differ, this section wins for form, section 4 for words.

**Rhythm of a section.** Label, then one statement, then one or two short paragraphs, then one action. Nothing is said twice. A readout or a clip may sit between the statement and the action. Softening: the statement names a thing you can do or find here, not a claim about the club.

**Labels** (eyebrows, row labels, readout labels, tab names). 1 to 3 words for a row or readout, 2 to 5 words for a section eyebrow, never above 40 characters. Written in sentence case in the source; the type system sets them in small mono capitals, so the words must be plain enough to survive capitals: "Today at the club", "Open until", "Ice bath". No full stop, no colon, no question mark. "&" is allowed inside a label, never in a sentence. The same category word repeats on every row of a list and never changes inside it. Softening: a label is a word the desk would use, never a data term ("Now", not "Status"; "Next class", not "Upcoming session").

**Headlines**, four forms, one length rule each:
- Hero line: 2 to 5 words in capitals, broken so every line is one unit (a verb, a noun phrase), no end punctuation; the full sentence stays in the h1 for screen readers. Softening: warm verbs and the word "together", never a command at the reader.
- Section statement: one sentence of 7 to 12 words in sentence case, subject-first or verb-first, no end punctuation; a series of three parallel verb phrases with commas and a final "and" is the house rhythm. Softening: the subject is "you" or a room or a person, not "we".
- Oversized caps title (a stat band, a pillar opener): 2 to 5 words, one or two words per line, may be cropped at the edge. Pillar names (Elite Nutrition, Elite Training, Elite Rest) fit this form as given.
- Item title (a card, a step, a room): 2 to 6 words in sentence case, a noun phrase or a quiet imperative, no full stop.

**Body.** A paragraph is two sentences, about 28 words, never above 50. A sentence runs 2 to 20 words; the beat is a short setup then a longer payoff ("Classes are included. Book one in the app or at the front desk and the hour is yours."). Two or three clipped fragments are allowed for practical reassurance only, with facts FACTS.md carries ("Towels provided. Lockers too."), never for attitude. "Not A, but B" is allowed once per page at most and only about this place, never about other gyms. About two "you" for every "we". Questions: at most one per page, in a lead, the kind the desk would ask ("Want a tour?"); none in headlines. Punctuation: full stops, commas, one spaced dash per section at most (prefer a new short sentence), en dash for ranges, curly apostrophes, no exclamation marks, no colons in prose, no ellipsis except inside a trimmed quote as the data gives it.

**Buttons.** Imperative verb first, 2 to 5 words, no punctuation, the object names the thing you get or open. One primary phrase repeated unchanged wherever the sale is offered: "Buy a pass". Per-card buttons are unique by carrying the tier and the length ("Get Elite for 4 weeks", tone only). The hero button is the sale itself, by Raoul's instruction (the Lab's hero takes a goal verb; v3 does not). Softening verbs: buy, get, book, see, message, ask; never claim, unlock, start your journey, join the movement. Secondary actions are short and plain ("See all classes", "Message us").

**Numbers.** A number is a readout: label above, the figure the largest thing in its block, no sentence around it. Readouts take 24-hour time ("22:00"), temperature with its unit and an en dash ("4–6 °C"), the rating with its count on one line ("4.7 · 488 reviews"). Prices in IDR keep the dot as thousands separator ("IDR 350.000"), a deliberate departure from the Lab, whose figures never pass 999. No "+" after a count: every Elite Fit count is exact. Counters: zero-padded two digits ("01", "02") on a short ordered list, none on class rows, where the time is the index. In body text, numbers stay out of paragraphs except a time, a price or a length that the sentence is about; written as digits.

**Captions and quotes.** A clip or image caption is one line, title left and a fact right ("Pool deck · open until 22:00"), no description. A review shows the name, the stars and the quote verbatim from reviews.json, trimmed only where the data already trims it, with curly quotation marks; one at a time. A coach line is first name, then the classes the timetable gives, joined by commas, nothing else.

**Case and type are not the writer's job.** Write every string in sentence case with normal spacing; the type system sets capitals and tracking. Never type a string in capitals for emphasis.
