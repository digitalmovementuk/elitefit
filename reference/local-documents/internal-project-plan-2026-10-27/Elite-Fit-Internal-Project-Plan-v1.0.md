# EliteFit — Internal Project Plan

**Version 1.0 · Pre-planning draft · Internal coordination**

## 1. What this plan is for

This is a starting framework for organising the EliteFit website project. It helps the team see who handles what, which pieces need to move together and what must be decided before detailed delivery planning. It is not an agreed schedule, a final scope or a record of completed work.

The proposed website period is four months, **November 2026–February 2027**. The expected scope is **12–15 core editable WordPress pages**; the page list and approvals still need agreement. The existing October prototype is visual input. Its checkout is an unconnected demo.

The two main customer journeys are buying class/day passes and signing up for monthly membership. We need to clarify whether “monthly” means a fixed-term purchase, automatic renewal or both.

Keep website production separate from the commercial six-month Phase 1, which includes support and initial SEO. New SEO research and production start after website approval: the first ten pages are planned for March–April, with the later SEO phase in May–October. Technical website SEO and migration checks can happen during the build.

## 3. The workstreams that run together

**Requirements and content:** Raoul prioritises requirements; Patrick supplies and verifies offers, membership rules and operating facts. An assigned content lead drafts original copy, Raoul reviews quality and the client approval lead approves. Betzy gathers missing inputs. Agree the core pages and exclusions; approve content in page batches so build and remaining copy can progress together.

**Website design and build:** Mohammed uses the prototype as an input, develops the agreed direction and builds reusable, editable WordPress pages. Review representative pages before applying the approach across the full site.

**Integrations:** start this alongside website planning, not after the design is finished. Map the website, membership, booking, payment and confirmation systems. Evaluate the existing GymMaster portal before custom development. Verify actual account capabilities, access, provider, currency, any QRIS requirement and support.

The current proposal includes integration advice, requirements and vendor coordination. Connection implementation, payment setup and CRM automation need separately agreed scope and cost. Café POS/Moka and marketing automation become launch dependencies only if required for the main customer journey; otherwise keep them for later.

**Operations and quality:** involve Patrick throughout so customer records, staff actions and support needs shape the build. Website quality and technical SEO sit within delivery; new SEO content production remains a separate follow-on.

The phases show sequence and overlap, not fixed deadlines. Review availability, holiday cover, workload and implementation scope before assigning dates. The separate March–April support and initial SEO period should not become a reason to leave essential purchase functionality unfinished.

## 5. Working process and useful checkpoints

Use a small number of checkpoints to decide whether the next stage is ready:

- **Validated requirements:** the core pages, customer journeys, sale rules and scope boundaries are understood and agreed.
- **Feasible connected journey:** the selected systems can support the required flow, with an agreed implementer and commercial scope.
- **Design approval:** representative pages and the purchase flow have been reviewed; final purchase-flow approval follows a connected test.
- **Working build:** the agreed pages and connections work together, with remaining issues visible.
- **Launch readiness:** staff can use the service, important checks pass and the required approvals are explicit.

Later testing should cover devices, accessibility, failed or repeated transactions, payment/member-record consistency and customer confirmations. Prepare staff support, backups and rollback ownership, then verify the actual public domain, favicon and social preview after release. Detail the test checklist when the solution is clearer.

Use sandbox accounts and test data. Real bookings or payments require Raoul’s explicit approval. Public release needs an explicit Go; sitemap submission needs its own separate approval. Passing a checkpoint or receiving no reply is not approval.

## 6. Coordination, feedback and changes

Betzy maintains one shared task board and one feedback list. A light proposed rhythm is a daily async update while delivery is active, plus a weekly planning/demo/client update. Agree the frequency and review expectations at kickoff rather than treating them as existing commitments.

Each work package has one accountable owner, helpers where useful, dependencies and an expected outcome. Keep “waiting for a decision” visible. Mark work complete only when there is evidence and the required review is finished.

For feedback, capture the page or journey, the issue and the requested outcome. Raoul distinguishes a fix against the agreed scope from a new request. The affected delivery owner explains any cost or timing effect before the scope changes. Betzy records the decision in one place so conflicting comments do not become separate instructions.

## 7. Open decisions and dependencies

| Question to resolve | Coordination owner | Why it matters |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Who gives routine client approval, and when are both owners needed? | Betzy, with Putu and Richard | Keeps decisions clear. |
| Which pages, offers and membership rules belong in scope? | Raoul, with Patrick and the owners | Sets the content and purchase requirements. |
| What systems and access already exist, and what can they actually support? | Raoul, with client system owners | Determines the feasible integration route. |
| Who implements the connections, at what agreed scope and cost? | Raoul | Removes the main ownership gap. |
| Who owns and writes the original website copy? | Raoul | Assigns drafting responsibility and supports page-batch approvals. |
| Are POS/Moka or marketing automation necessary for the core journey? | Raoul, with Patrick | Prevents optional work from blocking the website. |
| What review capacity, holiday cover and later support are available? | Betzy | Makes later planning realistic. |

The main dependency chain is requirements → feasible systems → agreed implementation → connected test → final purchase-flow approval → working build → acceptance. Content collection and website foundations can progress alongside it.

For each open decision, record its owner, status, eventual decision date and effect on scope or sequence. The main planning risks are missing access, unclear integration ownership, late content/decisions and unsupported payment behaviour. Resolve these early rather than adding more reporting.
