01 — What is included today

There is a clear scope gap.

Earlier project notes record Patrick’s goals: make it easy to buy a day pass or four-week membership, keep customer details for follow-up, and connect the online shop with WhatsApp or SMS messages. The latest proposal does not include most of that setup.

01

Already in the website proposal

12–15 WordPress pages, forms, approved booking or pass links, supplier-provided booking boxes, advice on connections, testing guidance, launch support and handover.

02

Needs a separate agreement

Shop and payment setup, customer follow-up records, automatic WhatsApp or SMS messages, and custom connections between systems.

Recommendation: agree and price the connection work by December. Build it with the website in January, then test it before the planned February launch. Without the software names and vendor support, we should not promise a fixed build price or guaranteed connection.

02 — The customer journey

Make each next step easy to follow.

The goal is a connected route from the first website visit to the right booking, payment confirmation and follow-up.

01 · FINDRead about a class, day pass or membership
02 · CHOOSEUse the order option, ideally on the website
03 · PAYPay securely and receive confirmation
04 · FOLLOW UPRecord the choice and send an agreed message
Keep card details with ESB or its payment provider. If ESB cannot keep checkout on the website, ask Patrick to approve a link to ESB’s ordering page. If a provider cannot send purchase or visit updates automatically, agree a staff-led fallback or defer that step.

04 — Working timeline

Five milestones to launch and handover.

This sequence matches the proposal’s November 2026–April 2027 build and support period. Name the systems and check ESB’s checkout options in November; approve the chosen route and added work in December so it can be built and tested before the February launch target.

  1. 01
    November 2026

    List the systems

    Name each platform, its owner and vendor. Confirm the exact ESB product and whether website checkout is a preference or a must-have. Ask ESB what secure checkout it can provide. Agree the visitor journey and day-pass rule.

    Output: named tools, owners, checkout preference and open questions.

  2. 02
    December 2026

    Confirm the build

    Ask ESB to confirm whether a checkout box or website connection is available and what it costs. Compare that with its separate ordering page. Patrick chooses the route. Agree the added scope and price.

    Output: approved checkout route, connection plan, owners, added price and dates.

  3. 03
    January 2027

    Build the journeys

    Build the website and approved checkout route in a test setup. Use an ESB checkout box or connection if supported; otherwise use the separate ESB page Patrick approved. Vendors handle settings only they can change.

    Output: website and order journeys ready to test.

  4. 04
    February 2027

    Test and launch

    Test the approved checkout route on phones and computers, including successful, failed and cancelled payments, confirmation, customer record, permission and follow-up message.

    Output: completed test record and approval for the 28 February 2027 target, if vendor support and reviews are on time.

  5. 05
    March–April 2027

    Support and hand over

    Resolve agreed launch issues, train the team on routine updates and document remaining vendor tasks.

    Output: guide and handover by 30 April.

05 — Scope and deliverables

Separate today’s work from the added build.

The current proposal covers the website and advice on connections. The recommended add-on would name every connection and result before implementation starts.

Already included

Website and advice

  • 12–15 editable WordPress pages
  • Forms and agreed visitor routes
  • Approved booking links or supplier-provided boxes; confirm whether ESB supplies an in-website checkout box
  • Connection requirements, vendor notes, testing advice and roadmap
  • Website testing, launch support and handover

Add-on to agree

Working connections

  • Named booking, shop/payment and customer follow-up tools, including the ESB checkout option
  • Day-pass and four-week membership paths with the approved checkout route
  • Agreed customer detail and permission handoff
  • Specified WhatsApp/SMS message paths
  • Test record and staff handover guide

06 — Launch checks

Prove the path works before launch.

Each agreed journey should be tried from start to finish with test details before Elite Fit approves it.

A visitor reaches the correct booking or shop page from a phone and a computer.
The order and payment stay on the website if ESB supports it. If checkout must move to ESB, Patrick approves that route first.
A completed payment creates the right confirmation; failed or cancelled payments are clearly shown and are not treated as sales.
The day-pass credit is applied to the membership if Elite Fit confirms that rule.
The customer record shows the right purchase and contact permission where supported.
Only people who agreed to messages receive the correct channel and message.
The team knows the fallback if a provider connection is unavailable.
Each issue has a named owner and is checked again after the fix.
