# Elite Fit Bali — Website connections: feasibility and delivery plan

**Prepared 21 October 2026 · Working plan for review**

## Main finding

The goals recorded for Patrick are achievable in principle: help a visitor choose a day pass or four-week membership, pay online, record the customer’s choice, and send the right follow-up. The exact build cannot be confirmed until Elite Fit names the booking, shop/payment, customer follow-up and messaging systems.

There is a material scope gap. The current proposal includes advice about connections and the use of existing approved booking links or supplier-provided booking boxes. It says the actual setup of customer records, payments, WhatsApp/SMS messages and custom connections needs a separate agreement. Those build tasks are not currently included or priced.

A new message from Mo, the tech lead, raises a more specific checkout preference: he asked whether ordering and payment should happen on the website or send visitors to ESB’s ordering system. The reply was: **“Ideally in our website.”** Treat this as the preferred experience, not yet a confirmed must-have from Patrick. The exact ESB product and its checkout options are still unknown.

**Recommendation:** agree a separately priced connection package by December 2026, then build and test it alongside the website. If the platform names and vendor support are confirmed early, the target website launch of 28 February 2027 remains workable. That date is a planning target, not a confirmed commitment.

## Checkout preference raised by Mo

Mo’s question shows the choice clearly: a visitor either completes the order and payment on Elite Fit’s website, or moves to ESB’s ordering system. The preference is to keep the journey on the website. That is feasible if ESB supplies a secure checkout box for the page, or a supported way for the website to place an order and receive its result. The payment itself should still be handled by ESB or its payment provider; the website should not store card details.

If ESB only allows a separate ordering page, that is the simpler fallback, but Patrick should approve it before it is used. A special connection built for Elite Fit may also be possible, but it needs a separate check, quote and vendor support. The supplied messages do not confirm which option ESB supports.

## What Patrick’s earlier goals ask the journey to do

1. A visitor finds Elite Fit, sees the offer and can buy a day pass or four-week membership without a long detour.
2. The day-pass purchase and membership are handled through the club’s online shop or booking provider.
3. A customer follow-up tool records the person and what they bought, with permission to contact them.
4. Agreed welcome and post-visit messages encourage a first visit and, where appropriate, a longer membership. Earlier notes say SMS should be used for Australian visitors.
5. The club’s existing rule that the day-pass price can be deducted from a four-week membership is reflected correctly. Elite Fit should confirm that rule before setup.

These needs come from earlier Elite Fit planning notes. The material reviewed contains no names for the actual software platforms.

## Feasibility by connection

| Visitor need | Initial view | What must be confirmed |
|---|---|---|
| Open the approved timetable or booking page from the website | **High** if the provider supplies a working link. The proposal already allows approved links. | Final booking destination, mobile behaviour and who maintains it. |
| Show a provider’s booking box on the website | **Likely** if the provider supplies a supported box that works on phones. It may fit the current offer if supplied and approved. | Provider instructions, page placement, mobile display and any extra provider charge. |
| Keep day-pass and membership ordering and payment on the website, as preferred | **Medium / unknown** until ESB is identified. This works if ESB supplies a secure checkout box or supports an agreed website connection. | Exact ESB product, checkout options, payment confirmation, refunds, costs and the day-pass credit rule. If only a separate ESB page is possible, Patrick must approve that fallback. |
| Send purchase and contact details to the customer follow-up tool | **Medium** if the named tools have a ready-made connection. | Which details move, duplicate handling, customer permission and vendor charges. |
| Send automatic WhatsApp or SMS follow-up | **Medium** if the chosen tool supports the agreed message, country and permission. | Message timing, opt-in wording, unsubscribe route, Australian SMS, and who pays message fees. |
| Keep booking, purchase, attendance and membership changes in sync | **Unknown / higher effort**. This may need the platform vendors or separately priced custom work. | Whether vendors can share each update, extra fees, support owner and fallback process. |

**Overall feasibility: achievable as a phased project, conditional on the platform providers.** A booking link is low risk. Keeping payment on the website is possible only if ESB supports a secure checkout box or a suitable connection; that has not been confirmed. A separate ESB checkout is simpler, but needs Patrick’s approval because the current preference is to stay on the website. Purchase follow-up across several tools remains a separate, higher-risk item.

## The end-to-end customer path

**Website page → choose a class, day pass or membership → ESB order and secure payment shown on the website if supported → confirmation → customer follow-up tool records the choice and permission → agreed WhatsApp or SMS follow-up → Elite Fit reviews bookings and sales.**

If ESB cannot keep checkout on the website, ask Patrick to approve a link to ESB’s ordering page before using it. In either case, ESB or its payment provider should handle card details. If a provider cannot send a purchase or visit update automatically, agree a simple staff-led fallback or leave that step for a later phase.

## Scope and deliverables

### Already included in the current website proposal

- Design, copy and editable WordPress build for 12–15 agreed website pages.
- Website forms and the agreed visitor journeys.
- Existing approved booking/pass links or supplier-provided booking boxes. A standard ESB checkout box may fit if the provider supplies it and it needs no special connection work; confirm this before relying on it.
- Advice on what systems need to connect, who is responsible, vendor notes, testing advice and a connection roadmap.
- Website testing, launch support, two months of hypercare, an editing guide and handover.

### Needs a separate written scope and price

- Setting up the shop, memberships, payments or customer follow-up tool.
- Setting up a special ESB order/payment connection or sending purchase details between the website, shop and follow-up tool.
- Writing and configuring WhatsApp/SMS follow-up messages.
- Custom connections, vendor development, data imports or ongoing connection maintenance.
- Platform subscriptions, message charges and vendor fees.

### Recommended add-on deliverables

1. **Connection map:** which tools are used, what the visitor does, what information moves and who owns each step.
2. **Agreed journey and message plan:** day-pass and membership paths, the day-pass credit rule, permission wording and audience-specific follow-up.
3. **Built website paths:** the approved ESB checkout box or connection if supported; otherwise a separate ESB checkout only after Patrick approves it; plus any agreed customer-detail handoff.
4. **Configured follow-up:** only the number of message paths and tools listed in the signed add-on.
5. **Test record:** each agreed purchase and follow-up check marked pass or needs fixing, with phone and computer checks.
6. **Handover pack:** a short staff guide, vendor contacts, open issues and the owner of each ongoing task.

## Who does what

- **Elite Fit:** name the platforms; confirm prices, offers and the day-pass credit rule; choose a reviewer; approve messages; provide vendor contacts; and invite staff through each provider’s secure account-sharing process.
- **Digital Movement:** map the visitor journey, check what the providers support, build the agreed website parts, coordinate tests, record issues and hand over the guide.
- **Platform providers:** confirm their own capabilities and charges, supply working links or booking boxes, configure provider-only settings and fix issues inside their own systems.

No one needs to send passwords in a message. Give named people the access they need through each platform’s own invitation settings.

## Launch checks

The agreed launch path should pass these checks before approval:

- A visitor can reach the correct booking or shop page from a phone and a computer.
- The order and payment stay on the Elite Fit website if ESB supports it. If checkout must move to ESB, Patrick approves that route first.
- A completed payment creates the correct confirmation; a failed or cancelled payment is clearly shown and is not counted as a sale.
- The day-pass credit is applied to the four-week membership if Elite Fit confirms that rule.
- A customer record shows the correct purchase and contact permission, where the tools support it.
- Only people who have agreed to messages receive them; the right audience receives the agreed channel.
- A cancelled or failed payment is not treated as a completed purchase.
- The team knows what to do if a vendor connection is unavailable.

## Decisions needed before a fixed quote

1. What is the exact name of ESB’s ordering system, and who can answer questions about it?
2. Can ESB show a secure checkout directly on Elite Fit’s website and send back a clear payment result? What does it cost?
3. Is website checkout a must-have, or is a separate ESB checkout acceptable if needed? Patrick should decide.
4. Does Elite Fit already pay for the required shop, payment and follow-up features, or are new subscriptions needed?
5. Is the day-pass price still deducted from a four-week membership, and how should checkout show it?
6. Which purchase and visit details, messages and customer permissions need to be shared?
7. Who at Elite Fit and each provider can approve settings and take part in a test purchase?

Until these answers are known, the project can be planned but the connection build cannot be responsibly priced or guaranteed.
